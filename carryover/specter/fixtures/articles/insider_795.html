The Insider has obtained documents that detail Russian intelligence

operations across Central Europe over the past decade. The papers,

which include cable traffic between Moscow Centre and several embassies,

suggest a coordinated campaign to recruit assets in regulatory bodies

overseeing critical infrastructure. The trove was provided to The

Insider by a source inside the Federal Security Service who has

since left the country.

According to the documents, the campaign began in 2014 and accelerated

following the annexation of Crimea. Recruited assets — typically

mid-career bureaucrats with financial pressures — were paid through

shell companies registered in Cyprus and Belize. Payments ranged from

several thousand euros monthly for low-value targets to six-figure

sums for officials with direct access to sensitive procurement

decisions.

Several of the names identified in the documents have been

cross-referenced with prior reporting by Bellingcat and OCCRP. In at

least three cases, the individuals named held senior positions in

agencies responsible for inspecting nuclear power plants and gas

transit infrastructure. None of those individuals has responded to

requests for comment.

The Federal Security Service did not respond to a request for comment

sent through its official press contact. A spokesperson for the

Russian foreign ministry dismissed the documents as fabrications,

without addressing any specific claim.

The Insider's reporting forms part of a broader investigation in

collaboration with Der Spiegel and the BBC, which will be published in

the coming weeks. Readers can submit additional information securely

via SecureDrop.
